About the project

Open weights are
not the whole source.

05 / ABOUT

A model without its training history asks everyone downstream to trust what they cannot inspect. OpenWALDO exists to make that history reviewable, verifiable, and portable.

The case

Open source replaced trust in software with the right to inspect, rebuild, and change it. AI needs the same foundation.

The missing layer of open AI is the source material.

Weights are executable artifacts, not a complete account of how a model came to exist. Without named sources, asserted licenses, exact object identity, and training records, “open” stops at distribution.

WALDO makes training material behave more like open-source input: named, reviewable, versioned, attributable, content-addressed, and usable without trusting a central data service. The public index is the map. Federated lookaside storage serves the canonical bytes. BOMs carry the resolved evidence into exports and model releases.

What’s missing isn’t another trust pledge. It’s an inspectable source of data and a toolchain that keeps the evidence attached.

A commons, not a product

No single company should own the substrate for open AI. The corpus is governed through ordinary Git review; contributors host content-addressed objects; consumers can verify or export the material themselves. WALDO records attributable facts and the limits of what it verified—it does not turn a hash into a legal or safety conclusion.

The project is designed so that governance, storage, verification, and model work remain explicit rather than disappearing behind a service.

02

03

Claims stay falsifiable

Sources and licenses are attributable assertions open to correction and review.

04

Limits stay visible

Unsupported formats and incomplete disclosures fail visibly instead of being inferred.
