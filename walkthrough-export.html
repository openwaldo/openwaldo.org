howto · waldo export

Export the data. Bring your own trainer.

What this HOWTO does: takes the corpus out of the index as verifiable parquet and trains it with your own stack — PyTorch, TorchTitan, or anything that reads parquet — with EXPORT.json carrying the pedigree. Nothing about the index requires you to use waldo as a trainer; its job is to be a trustworthy source of data.

All you need is the waldo binary and a checkout of the index — exporting needs no account, no credentials, and no Python.

WALDO export

Take the corpus out as parquet and train it with your own stack.

step 1

Export

Shards export as they are — parquet, ZSTD-compressed, byte-identical to what the manifest hashes. That matters: every exported file verifies against the index on its own, so a collaborator can confirm you did not quietly edit the data. --jsonl decodes to canonical JSONL instead, at roughly 3–4× the disk and producing files the index has no hash for.

export
$ waldo index export core/books ~/corpus
exporting 1 shard(s) as shard: 6 docs, 510.0K tokens, 2.3 MB on disk
  core/books/books-87f1a41d56b5.parquet (2.3 MB, 6 docs)
exported 1 file(s) (0 already present) + EXPORT.json → ~/corpus

# filter by license profile — the index labels, the consumer chooses
$ waldo index export science --license "CC-BY-*" ~/corpus

# or stream a subset without materializing it at all
$ waldo index export core/books --jsonl --stdout | head -1 | jq -r '.text' | head -3
step 2

Know the schema

One row per document — text (the document; text, never tokens), sha256 (the dedup key), license (SPDX token; constant within a shard, since shards are single-license), license_raw (what upstream said, when it differs — the evidence for the label), source / source_name, lang / lang_score, and date, tokens, kind, meta. EXPORT.json is the BOM: the index commit, and for every file its path, URL, sha256, license, doc and token counts.

step 3

Train with PyTorch

Read it with pyarrow or datasets; nothing is waldo-specific. Two habits worth keeping. Filter on license, not on the path — a subtree can mix licenses across shards, and the column is the authority per record. And keep EXPORT.json next to the data: it is what lets anyone re-derive which index commit your training run consumed, which is the whole reason to take data from here rather than from a scrape.

with PyTorch
import pyarrow.parquet as pq, glob

files = sorted(glob.glob("~/corpus/core/books/*.parquet"))
tbl = pq.read_table(files, columns=["text", "license", "lang"])

# shards are single-license, so filtering is a cheap column scan — this is
# how a downstream consumer honours "no copyleft" or "permissive only"
mask = pc.is_in(tbl["license"], value_set=pa.array(["CC0-1.0", "CC-BY-4.0"]))
step 4

Or generate a TorchTitan job bundle

For multi-node runs, waldo generates a job bundle rather than trying to drive your cluster: config.toml (a torchtitan job config carrying your architecture, optimizer and step count — it says of itself that it is “a STARTING POINT, not turnkey”), shards.txt (the materialized shard paths, in training order), and JOB.md (submission notes and the caveats, including which torchtitan model family is nearest and where waldo’s architecture differs from it).

torchtitan bundle
$ waldo model train titan1b core/books --steps 1000 --backend torchtitan
materialized 1 shard(s): 6 docs, ~510031 tokens
job bundle written: ~/.waldo/models/titan1b/jobs/titan-20260804-012200
  — see JOB.md there for cluster submission
no training run recorded — record it after the cluster run completes

Note the last line: no run is recorded. waldo did not train anything, so it does not claim a run. Record it after the cluster job finishes, or the BOM chain will have a gap where the training was. Single-node multi-GPU does not need any of this — that is the PyTorch backend and it is turnkey: waldo compose build configs/words373m.yaml --gpus 8.
