01 / CORPUS

OpenWALDO puts source claims, asserted licenses, conversion identity, canonical object inventory, exact counts, and Git history on a public record that can be inspected and challenged.

Why use OpenWALDO

The record travels
with the data.

Scale matters. Accountability matters more. The index separates attributable claims from machine-verifiable identity so downstream users can see what is recorded, what can be verified, where its history lives, and where the evidence ends.

The public index currently records 20 corpora across 1,051 canonical shards—75.1M documents and 124.0B reference tokens under 18 asserted license identifiers.

01 / ORIGIN

Source provenance

Where the material came from, how it was acquired, and which source claims were placed on the record.

02 / RIGHTS

License accountability

Attributed license assertions that can be inspected, questioned, and corrected—not silent legal conclusions.

03 / IDENTITY

Reproducible objects

Conversion identity, canonical shards, content hashes, and exact counts bind the record to specific bytes.

04 / HISTORY

Public governance

Meaning changes through Git, leaving a reviewable history instead of disappearing behind a private catalog.

Recorded is not the same as verified.

A manifest records an attributable claim. A hash proves object identity. Verification proves only the checks that were actually run. None of them alone establishes legal rights, quality, or safety.

Choose your proof

WALDO separates quick structural checks from full object and record validation, so the cost of verification is explicit.

01

Verify metadata

Validate index structure and check that referenced objects are reachable with their declared sizes.

waldo index verify core/books
02

Verify objects

Download selected objects and prove every content hash.

waldo index verify core/books --objects
03

Audit records

Validate canonical records, identities, text hashes, duplicates, and manifest totals.

waldo index audit core/books
