# What open source has taught me

Hi, I'm Greg.

For more than two and a half decades, I've helped build open-source
projects and communities that became important infrastructure around the world.

I created [Warewulf](https://warewulf.org/), co-founded
[CentOS](https://www.centos.org/), created Singularity—now
[Apptainer](https://apptainer.org/), and founded
[Rocky Linux](https://rockylinux.org/) and then the [RESF](https://www.resf.org/). I
also created [OpenELA](https://openela.org/) with Oracle and SUSE to
preserve access to the sources that Enterprise Linux depends upon.

Across these efforts, I have brought vision, collaboration, and structure to
communities so people could come together around a common purpose. What began
as an idea often became something far greater than any of us expected, and, in
several cases, completely changed the world of technology.

OpenWALDO begins with that same belief: build a shared foundation, a meaningful
vision, and a way for anyone to participate.

Together we will build something extraordinary!

# A foundation we can share

Every AI builder needs a broad foundation of training material. Today,
organizations invest enormous effort collecting, preparing, organizing,
licensing, storing, and maintaining much of the same foundational data.

There is a tremendous opportunity to build that common layer together and
scale it far beyond what any company could build alone.

A shared, community-driven corpus would give model builders a strong starting
point while allowing them to focus more resources on their greatest value:
model architecture, training methods, specialized knowledge, safety,
performance, and their unique differentiators.

This is the goal of OpenWALDO, a place where we can build together, work
together, exchange ideas, learn, solve shared problems, and help one another
understand, build, and operate AI models and infrastructure.

New participants can learn by contributing and being part of the community.
Companies, institutions, and individuals can work alongside one another on a
foundation that benefits everyone, while learning how to build models from
the ground up.

Collaborating on the foundation gives everyone more room to learn, innovate,
and build above it.

# A community corpus of training data

OpenWALDO is both a community where people help and learn from one another and
a community-managed corpus of AI training material, with the toolchain needed
to contribute, review, verify, and begin training with the community corpus.

WALDO stands for Weights, Artifacts, Licenses, Data, and Origins. In my mind,
this is the definition of what a truly open source model needs to provide,
but there is still one major missing piece for the spirit of open source...
Community.

# The community corpus itself

The OpenWALDO index records where training material came from, which rights were
asserted, who contributed it, and exactly which bytes belong to each version.
Models trained from the corpus can carry an AI Bill of Materials identifying
the sources used to build them.

The public index is in Git, which provides the provenance and management.
The large data objects (shards) live in federated lookaside storage and are
verified by checksums. Contributors sign off under the Developer Certificate
of Origin and submit their work for community review.

These are proven scalable open-source mechanisms adapted for AI training data.

# The community builds the OpenWALDO corpus

OpenWALDO becomes more valuable every time someone contributes knowledge,
improves the tools, reviews the evidence, shares expertise, or helps another
participant.

Model builders and researchers can help define what useful training material
looks like.

Companies can contribute public documentation, product information, research,
and other knowledge they want represented in future models.

Data experts, librarians, legal specialists, and reviewers can strengthen
organization, licensing evidence, provenance, and quality.

Infrastructure engineers can help the index, storage, verification, and
training systems operate at massive scale.

Students, hobbyists, homelab builders, and people learning about AI can
participate, help host distributable data from other sources, ask questions,
share knowledge, and grow with the project.

We work together through community discussions, Git review, transparent
decisions, DCO accountability, and shared technical development. Every
participant can see the record, contribute improvements, learn from others,
and benefit from what the community builds.

# Why now

Competition and necessity are driving extraordinary progress in AI. It
belongs in the areas where companies and researchers create their greatest
differentiation.

The base corpus is different. It is foundational infrastructure that every
model builder can leverage universally and collaboratively.

This is our opportunity to build the shared foundation of AI together.
A large and diverse community can assemble tremendous amounts of knowledge,
drive accountability, reduce duplicated effort, and create a better
starting point for everyone.

Open source has demonstrated this model across operating systems, cloud
infrastructure, scientific computing, networking, and the internet itself.

Now we can bring it to AI.

# Build it with us

For more than two and a half decades, I have helped turn ambitious open-source
ideas into communities, technologies, and infrastructure that changed
industries—and, in meaningful ways, changed the world.

I brought vision, leadership, and structure. The communities brought their
experience, creativity, commitment, and willingness to build together. That
combination allowed us to create far more than any one person or organization
could have built alone.

I believe OpenWALDO can become that kind of community for AI.

My role is to provide the vision, establish the structure, and help build a
community where people and organizations can contribute meaningfully.
OpenWALDO will become extraordinary through the researchers, model builders,
data experts, infrastructure engineers, companies, reviewers, educators,
students, hobbyists, and community leaders who join us.

Whether you build models, curate data, operate infrastructure, study licensing
and provenance, teach others, or are simply excited to learn, there is a place
for you in OpenWALDO.

Let's build the common foundation together, learn from one another, make life
easier for everyone developing AI, and create something greater than any of us
could create alone.
