// about the project

Open source is how the world builds what it trusts.

OpenWALDO exists to give AI the property every other layer of critical infrastructure already has: a shared, collaborative foundation that anyone can obtain, inspect, and contribute to.

the founder

Founded by Gregory Kurtzer.

GitHub LinkedIn X

Greg has spent his entire career building open source — not just software, but the communities that carry it. He co-founded CentOS, the freely available enterprise Linux that became one of the most widely deployed operating systems on the planet. He created Warewulf, the cluster provisioning system that has run high-performance computing installations for over two decades. He created Singularity — now the Linux Foundation’s Apptainer — which brought containers to scientific and high-performance computing and became the standard way research computing runs reproducible workloads. And when CentOS changed course in 2020, he founded Rocky Linux and the Rocky Enterprise Software Foundation, rebuilding a community of millions around the promise that enterprise Linux would stay free — a project that went from an announcement in a GitHub comment to one of the fastest-growing operating systems in the world.

That instinct was formed over nearly two decades in high-performance computing at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, supporting scientific computing for UC Berkeley and the Department of Energy — where the software that mattered was open, and the science that mattered was reproducible. The pattern across all of it: find the place where openness is missing, build the foundation, and grow the community that makes it permanent.

Why OpenWALDO

Greg is a huge advocate and supporter of AI. That’s exactly why this project exists. Look at the arguments consuming the field — accusations over model distillation, creators unhappy about their work being trained on without consent, companies unable to see what’s inside the models they depend on. Every one of those problems is downstream of the same cause: the data is closed. Make the data genuinely open source, and the problems don’t get argued away — they disappear.

Open weights is not open source. What AI needs is what every layer of trusted infrastructure before it needed: a shared, collaborative base foundation — a corpus of knowledge that anyone can obtain and anyone can contribute to, with licenses, provenance, and the right to be removed built into the format itself. Open source is necessary. It is how strangers cooperate at scale, how trust gets built without permission, and how technology ends up serving everyone instead of a few.

So he’s doing what he has always done: founding the open, shared foundation — this time, a corpus of AI training data — and inviting everyone to join. The invitation is real, and it’s open.
