// about the project

Open source is how the world builds what it trusts.

OpenWALDO exists to give AI the property every other layer of critical infrastructure already has: a shared, collaborative foundation that anyone can obtain, inspect, and contribute to.

the mission

A shared foundation of knowledge — owned by everyone, and by no one.

OpenWALDO is building the thing AI is missing: an open source corpus of training data — a commons of licensed, provenance-tracked knowledge — and the toolchain that turns it into models anyone can audit, verify, and rebuild. Not a product, not a platform, and not any one company’s asset: a base foundation the whole field can stand on, the way it already stands on Linux, on open compilers, and on open protocols.

Look at the arguments consuming AI — accusations over model distillation, creators unhappy about their work being trained on without consent, companies unable to see inside the models they depend on. Every one of those problems is downstream of the same cause: the data is closed. Make the data genuinely open source — licensed, attributed, removable, on the record — and those problems don’t get argued away. They disappear.

Open weights is not open source. What AI needs is what every layer of trusted infrastructure before it needed: a shared, collaborative base foundation — a corpus of knowledge that anyone can obtain and anyone can contribute to — with licenses, provenance, and the right to be removed built into the format itself.

And the name? For years, everyone has been asking the same question about training data — where is it, where did it come from, who said you could use it. This project exists so the answer can finally, cheerfully, be: “Here’s WALDO.” Stripes and all.
how the project runs

In the open, like everything worth trusting.

Everything happens in git

The corpus is curated by pull request; every contribution carries a DCO sign-off under the contributor’s own name. There is no side channel — the repository history is the project’s history.

Peer review is the gate

The license bar is enforced by human review, the way every serious open source project keeps itself honest — license claims checked, sources verified, provenance recorded.

Nobody owns the bytes

Training data is hosted by contributors themselves on federated, content-addressed storage. The project holds the map, not the territory — no single point of control, or failure.

Funding-honest, laptop-first

The roadmap says plainly what each stage costs: today’s rungs train on laptops and single GPUs; the foundation model will take partners. No vaporware, no pretending compute is free.

the founder

Founded by Gregory Kurtzer. Built by everyone who shows up.

GitHub LinkedIn X

Greg has spent his career founding open source projects and the communities that carry them: co-founder of CentOS, creator of Warewulf and of Singularity (now the Linux Foundation’s Apptainer), and founder of Rocky Linux and the Rocky Enterprise Software Foundation — communities counted in the tens of millions, built on the promise that essential infrastructure stays free. That instinct comes from nearly two decades in high-performance computing at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, where the software that mattered was open and the science that mattered was shared.

The pattern across all of it: find the place where openness is required, build the foundation, and grow the community that makes it permanent. As a huge advocate of AI, Greg sees that place clearly — the data. So he’s doing what he has always done: creating the open, shared foundation, and inviting everyone to join. The invitation is real, and it’s open.
